> **Main idea:** Pre-workout nutrition is a tool for training quality, not a requirement for muscle growth. Your body does not need food at a specific "anabolic window" — what matters most is total daily intake.

---

# Part 1 — Carbohydrates Before Resistance Training

## 1. The core question: do you need pre-workout carbs?

For most resistance-training sessions, **no**. Stored muscle glycogen is generally sufficient.

During a typical session, total muscle glycogen decreases by around **25–40%**. You are using glycogen, but you are not emptying the tank in one workout.

```
Eat carbs throughout the day
        ↓
Glycogen stores fill up
        ↓
Train
        ↓
Use stored glycogen
```

Not:

```
Eat carbs 30–60 min before gym
        ↓
Otherwise you have no fuel
```

---

## 2. When pre-workout carbs do become useful

Carbohydrates are rarely a performance limiter during **short or moderate** sessions. But they can matter more in certain situations:

| Situation | Pre-workout carbs useful? |
|-----------|--------------------------|
| Short / moderate session, fed state | Generally not necessary |
| Long session (>~45 min) or high volume (>8–10 sets) | May help maintain training volume |
| Training fasted | Eating beforehand may help |
| Calorie deficit | Worth considering, especially for high-volume work |

> The more demanding the session and the less favorable your fueling situation, the more value pre-workout carbs may provide.

---

## 3. How carbs before training help (when they do)

Even when stored glycogen is adequate, carbs eaten beforehand can:

- provide glucose from the bloodstream during the workout
- help spare or augment glycogen availability
- be especially useful when the session is long or glycogen stores are lower than normal

They are not automatically required, but they are not useless either.

---

## 4. The calorie-deficit case

When in a calorie deficit, glycogen availability can be more relevant:

```
Calorie restriction
        ↓
Lower carbohydrate / energy availability
        ↓
Glycogen stores may be reduced
        ↓
Strength or training volume may suffer
        ↓
Pre-workout carbs become more worth considering
```

If your strength or volume is declining during a deficit, pay attention to whether pre-workout carbs make a difference for you.

---

# Part 2 — Protein Before Resistance Training

## 5. Do you need pre-workout protein for hypertrophy?

The conclusion here is much less exciting than most fitness advice suggests:

- Studies have **not** found a meaningful improvement in lean mass from protein consumed specifically before versus after training
- There was a small strength benefit in a couple of studies, but not enough to suggest an important pre-workout anabolic window

What actually matters:

| Factor | Importance |
|--------|-----------|
| Total daily protein | High — aim ~1.6–2.2 g/kg/day |
| Exact pre-workout protein timing | Optional |

> The anabolic window is not a sharp deadline. Total daily protein is the lever that matters.

---

# Part 3 — Practical Starting Points

## 6. By situation

**Short or moderate session:**
→ Pre-workout food is optional. Eat normally throughout the day.

**High-volume session:**
→ Carbs beforehand may help maintain training volume.

**Training fasted:**
→ Eating something before may help performance.

**Calorie deficit:**
→ Pre-workout carbs are more worth considering, especially if volume or strength is suffering.

---

## 7. Optional starting point for hypertrophy / hybrid training

If you want a concrete guideline to start from:

```
~0.5 g/kg carbohydrate
~0.3 g/kg protein
1–3 hours before training
```

This is a starting point, not a hard requirement.

---

# Mental Model

```
Low glycogen / fasted / long high-volume workout
               ↓
       Higher chance food helps

Fed / adequately fueled / normal moderate workout
               ↓
       Lower chance a specific pre-workout meal matters
```

Pre-workout nutrition **does not make or break** strength or hypertrophy outcomes. It is a small tuning variable, not a foundation.

---

# Key Takeaways

1. **Stored glycogen is usually enough** for a normal resistance-training session
2. **Pre-workout carbs matter more** when sessions are long, when training fasted, or when in a calorie deficit
3. **Pre-workout protein timing is not critical** — total daily protein is what drives hypertrophy
4. **Think of pre-workout food as a training quality tool**, not a muscle-building requirement
